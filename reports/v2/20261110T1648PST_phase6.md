### 2.1 AP1's seam: the correction is built and exact, and it cannot be applied to AP1 (runs `20261111T003616Z_seamfix_AP1`, job 51837337, and `20261111T004515Z_seamfix_AP1`, job 51838403)

**The seam.** The 345 interior cells of the checkpoint are recovered by the same classification (interior by two
cells, stored code differs from the steepest descent of the filled grid, not a tie within 1e-9): all on rows 389-390,
columns 2-1010, and the metre and feet recomputations give the same new code on every one of them (A1). The stored
codes are E 74, SE 56, S 9, SW 127, W 69, NW 6, N 1, NE 3; the steepest-descent codes are S 193, SW 42, SE 38, NE 34,
NW 29, N 6, W 3. The steepest drop exceeds the drop to the stored receiver by 0.029 m at the median and up to 0.27 m;
only 4 stored receivers are uphill or level. That already contradicts the checkpoint's reading of the seam: a block
boundary without a south comparison cannot produce the 192 south-pointing stored codes.

**The correction** follows the addendum's algorithm: the 345 cells are processed upstream first (stored area
ascending); each cell's area is subtracted along its old D8 path from the old receiver to the tile exit and added
along the new path, both followed in the current state of the direction grid, so that a seam cell downstream of
another is handled with its already-corrected area. One detail is needed to make this exact and is in the code and
the test: TopoAnalysis's `Area` processes cells from the highest down and hands each cell's total to its receiver
when the cell is processed, so a cell whose code points uphill adds its total to a receiver that has already passed
its own total on; the path updates therefore propagate only while the next cell is processed later, and the amount
moved is what the cell passed on (its area minus what such late donors returned to it, `passed_local`).

**Required test (2.1.6): pass.** `tests/test_seamfix.py` builds a 240 x 240 synthetic mosaic, routes it in two row
blocks with the slab convention so that two rows of the clipped 200 x 200 tile carry a planted seam (392 differing
interior cells, 66 of them pointing uphill), accumulates the area through the seam in TopoAnalysis's elevation order,
clips the tile, and asks the in-tile correction to reproduce the clipped area of the correctly routed mosaic:
**bit identity at dx = 1 m and 1e-9 of a cell at dx = 0.9752 m**, for the path algorithm and for an independent
recomputation (the inflow from outside the tile read off the stored grids, then the accumulation redone on the
corrected directions). A second test documents what the tile cannot see (2.1.3): with a mosaic in which a seam path
leaves the tile and re-enters it, the correction differs from the full recomputation exactly by the re-entered area,
at the re-entry cell and below it, and redoing the accumulation with the true inflow removes the residual.

**On AP1 the premise fails.** The algorithm, and the addendum's statement that "the stored `*_area` values at the 345
seam cells include all their upstream area", rest on the stored area having been accumulated through the stored
directions. That is checkable from the tile: at every interior cell the stored area must equal one cell plus what
its in-tile donors passed to it. It does, at every interior cell of AP1 except 699 on rows 389-391 (133 on 389, 565
on 390, 1 on 391), where the two disagree by 6 cells at the median, 185 at the 90th percentile and 5.9e4 at most,
positive and negative in equal measure (A1). The stored area is not consistent with the corrected directions either
(959 cells on rows 388-391). And the stored filled elevation itself carries the seam: the mean drop from row 389 to
390 is 0.174 m against 0.086-0.102 m between every other pair of rows from 385 to 395; per column the excess drop
runs from -0.23 to +0.36 m (5-95 %), median +0.087 m (0.29 ftUS), so it is not a constant offset, and raising the
rows below the seam by any constant between 0.01 and 0.15 m recovers at most half of the stored seam codes.
The three AP1 grids were therefore made with surfaces that differ from one another on those rows, which looks like
a stitching defect of the source processing (two source tiles or blocks joined with a misregistration of a row,
in different ways for the elevation and the routing); the mosaic is not on Sherlock to confirm it.

**Consequence.** Applied regardless, the correction moves amounts that are not what flowed through those cells, and
the result is not a drainage-area grid: 397 cells end below one cell, the minimum at -3.7e4 cells, on rows 382-401 and below,
and 9354 cells change in total (0.9 % of the tile; 669 rows touched along the paths; 245 cells with A >= 10^3 m^2
change by more than 1 %; the joint and 10^4 m^2 channel masks are unchanged, the stage-2 mask differs on 3 cells and
the 10^3 mask on 59). These numbers are the record of the first run (A1); they are not a correction. The in-tile
correction agrees with the independent recomputation to 2.3e-8 cells on AP1 (both trust the same stored area, so
the agreement proves the arithmetic, not the premise). The two grids `*_seamfix.tif` written by the first run are
invalid and are **not** in the manifest (the first run had added them; the manifest change was reverted); the AP1
config keeps the stored grids. The second run (`--no-write`) carries the consistency diagnostics in its record.

**Re-entry (2.1.3).** 338 of the 345 old paths and 343 of the new paths reach a tile exit (7 and 2 stop at an
uphill receiver); 1516 edge cells receive inflow from outside the tile, which is where any re-entering path would
hide. Moot for the correction, since it is not applied.

**What this means for Phase 6.** AP1 enters any rerun with grids that are mutually inconsistent on three rows, as
every v1 and v2 AP1 result so far has (the stored area is self-consistent with the stored directions on the other
1012 rows). Logged as N9 in `docs/numerics_changes.md`; options in section 9.
