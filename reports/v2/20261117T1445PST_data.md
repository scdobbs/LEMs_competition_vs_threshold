**Reading.**
- **The AP tiles were not cut from this file.** Their origins sit 0.15-0.98 of a cell off its lattice and their cells are
  0.018 % larger (0.97520 against 0.97502 m), so no window of this grid is the tile.
- **They carry the same values.** Nearest-neighbour sampling reproduces the tile bit for bit on 98.8-99.7 % of cells. Every
  cell that differs by more than 0.1 m has the tile higher than the parent, which is what pit filling does (the tiles are
  `filled` grids; the parent is not filled). The differing cells cluster in a few 100 × 100-cell blocks per tile (Appendix C2
  block counts), the pattern of filled depressions and of the switch-over cells where the two lattices pick different parent
  cells (0.3-1.2 % of cells, consistent with a 0.46-0.98 cell offset drifting by 0.18 cell across the tile).
- So the tiles and this grid are two nearest-neighbour warps of the same PAMAP source (the same facet values), made with
  slightly different warp parameters; the Appendix C2 bilinear rows (p05-p95 of Δz −0.25 to +0.20 m) measure only the
  resampling of facets, not a data difference.
- **Area outside the tiles.** The parent extends 2.2-32 km beyond each tile (west margins 2.2 km for AP3, 5.5 km for AP4;
  south margin 2.6 km for AP1; the rest 4-32 km) and has **no nodata inside any tile**. It is 57 % valid overall because
  **it is clipped to one watershed**: the valid area is a single drainage basin whose main stem runs east to the Monongahela
  valley at the north-east corner (`parent_overview/pamap_parent_overview.png`). AP2, AP4 and AP5 lie well inside it (no
  nodata within 1 km; 1-7 % within 2 km). **AP1 sits on the basin's south-east boundary** (18 % nodata within 500 m, 29 %
  within 1 km, 59 % of the east half of the 1 km window) and **AP3 on its western margin** (0.9 % within 500 m, 6.5 % within
  1 km, on the south and west sides). For a drainage-area computation on the full DEM (addendum 2 §6.5) a watershed clip is
  the right domain: nothing drains in across its boundary, so every catchment that reaches a tile is complete, provided the
  clip follows the true divide. Whether it does at AP1 and AP3 is a flow-routing check that was not run here; the areas
  stored in the AP tiles (`*_area.tif`) were computed on the full DEM and clipped (`docs/measurement_methods.md` §2), and
  this grid is the candidate for that provenance, not a verified match of it.

are complete (100 % valid cells; GM4: 9,998 of 10,000). The AP 30 m values bracket the AP tiles converted to metres within the
range a 30 m cell averages (e.g. AP1 326.8-433.0 against 324.5-439.1 m), so the AP grid is in metres on the same (NAVD88)
datum. **The GM 10 m grid is 30-34 m higher than the GM tiles** on every tile (GM1 328.9-455.8 against 295.2-424.7 m; GM4
164.1-307.9 against 134.9-273.4 m), the geoid-ellipsoid separation at Gabilan Mesa: the NCALM GM lidar, and therefore
`Gabilan_mesa.tif` and the GM tiles, are in ellipsoidal heights (*inferred*; gradients are unaffected).

**What Perron et al. (2012) say came from these grids** (quotes under 15 words or paraphrase; page numbers of the Nature PDF
and the SI PDF):
1. Methods, p. 4: the drainage network over the larger area, as "points with drainage area greater than a minimum value,
   Amin", pruned of spurs "less than 20 m (GM) or 30 m (AP) in length".
2. Methods, p. 4: A_min calibrated against the lidar valley network: "Amin = 10,000 m² for GM and 50,400 m² for AP".
3. Methods, p. 4: the networks "over larger geographic areas with the NED", Strahler orders, and each first- and second-order
   basin's drainage area; sample size "from tens of drainage basins to several thousand".
4. Fig. 1 (p. 1): the area distributions of first- and second-order basins (AP n = 6,721 and 1,376; GM n = 27,383 and 5,275)
   and the statement that "The smallest basins with tributaries are typically four times larger in the Allegheny Plateau".
5. Methods, p. 4: basin lengths from areas, "L ≈ √(3A)", hence the Péclet numbers Pe = (K/D)(3A)^(m+1/2).
6. Fig. 4 (p. 3): the Pe distributions, "Basins analysed are the same as in Fig. 1".
7. SI Fig. S2 (p. 3): first- and second-order basins "extracted from the coarser resolution NED" for the 25 km² AP test area,
   compared with the lidar valley network.

**What did not come from these grids:** K/D and m. Main text p. 3: "K/D and m have been estimated²¹ for representative sites
in each landscape from an independent analysis of the topography (Supplementary Table 2)"; SI Table S2 (p. 5) marks ∇²z_h,
D/K and m with "*Ref. 21" (Perron, Kirchner & Dietrich 2009). E is from ¹⁰Be (Methods, p. 4) and D = E/∇²z_h. So the coarse
grids set no transport coefficient in that paper; they set the network threshold A_min, the basin populations and L.

## 7. What the data can and cannot support

**The 3DEP point clouds can support a 1 m AP-3DEP grid and a crest-curvature measurement.**
- Class-2 density is 2.9-3.3 points/m² on every tile, with 97-99 % of 1 m cells holding at least one ground point and a
  median of 3. On the option (iii) hilltop pixels it is 2.9-3.3 points/m², the same as on the rest of the hillslope, with
  0.8-4.4 % empty cells; an 11 m curvature window (about 120 cells) averages 350-400 ground points, so C_ht at L_f can be
  measured on crests from the points themselves, not from a facet.
- It is clearly better than PAMAP on every stated number: QL2 against QL3, 0.71 m against 1.4 m nominal spacing (about 6×
  the ground-point density), 10 cm RMSEz specification, a stated compound CRS with both units and the geoid, and a
  single-day acquisition per tile. A measured PAMAP density cannot be given (no LAS on Sherlock).
- The surveys agree on AP1-AP4 to better than 0.1 m in the median after the feet-to-metres conversion, which also settles
  the v1 units question against an independent dataset.

**What they cannot support, or not yet.**
- **AP5.** The PAMAP-based AP5 grid and 3DEP disagree by 1.2 m in a banded pattern. Every AP5 result that rests on the PAMAP
  grid's absolute heights or on gradients across the bands (D₀, C_ht, the model fits) carries that uncertainty until the
  cause is placed in one survey or the other. Nothing here identifies which survey is wrong; the banding is the evidence.
- A 1 m grid is not yet made; its gridding method (binning of class-2 points, TIN, or a smoothed surface) is a decision
  (section 8), and the AP1 seam and the facet question can only be re-examined on that grid.
- The data cover the five tiles plus 500 m (6,766 ft squares) and nothing more: no drainage-area computation on a larger
  domain is possible from this download; the watershed-clipped PAMAP grid (section 3.1) remains the only full-DEM source.
- The TTV tile has no CRS record, no GPS time, no return or intensity attributes, 0.54-0.70 ground points/m², and 41 % of
  1 m cells with a ground point: a 1 m grid from it would be mostly interpolation; 2 m is the finest cell with 77 %
  occupancy. Its vertical datum is inferred, not stated. The second TTV tile named in the addendum is not on Sherlock, so
  whether the Montgomery & Dietrich area is covered in full cannot be settled from one tile and no polygon.
- The GM tiles are in ellipsoidal heights (inferred from the 30-34 m offset to the NED); harmless for the models, but any
  future comparison with an orthometric dataset over GM needs the geoid.
