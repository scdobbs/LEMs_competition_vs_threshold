- **acrit and nonlinear** (steps 2 and 5): the Phase 3 code reproduces the baseline's evaluation sequence and every
  misfit bit for bit (the cross-CPU 1e-13 noise seen in the forward gates does not appear here because the misfit
  is a mean over 1e6 cells of a solution computed by the same SuperLU on the same matrix). The directive's
  expectation holds.
- **joint** (step 4): the same 225 and 104 evaluations in the same order; the misfits differ by at most 8.5e-12
  relative, which is N6 (the channel profile scaled once instead of term by term; predicted <= 1e-14 of relief per
  evaluation, i.e. 1e-12 to 1e-11 in an MSE). No Nelder-Mead step differed: the largest per-evaluation difference
  never reached the 1e-4 tolerance that decides a simplex move.
- **ad** (step 3): the first six evaluations (the start point, its two finite-difference neighbours, the first
  projected step to the K and D upper bounds and its two neighbours) agree to 6.5e-8 in MSE (9e-14 relative), and
  from evaluation 6 on the parameters differ: by 2.7e-8 in log10 K at row 6 on AP5, 1.8e-8 on GM1, growing along
  the path. On GM1 both paths converge to the same basin and end within 1.9e-5 (K) and 5.5e-5 (D) of each other,
  both passing against v1; on AP5 the baseline path ends at v1's optimum (MSE 1005.3 after 108 evaluations) and the
  Phase 3 path in a different basin at MSE 2826 (117 evaluations, scipy's "relative reduction of F" stop), a
  point with K 5 times and D 185 times v1's.

The mechanism is N7 (`docs/numerics_changes.md`): step 3 computes `A**m` for all cells with numpy's vectorised
power, v1 with a scalar power per cell, and on the AMD Genoa nodes of `serc` (`CPU_GEN:SIE`, AVX-512) the two
round differently in the last bit for about 6 % of the arguments (235 of 3999 integers in a direct test, job
51705914). The advection entries then differ by one ulp, the solution by ~3e-16 relative, the misfit by ~1e-13
relative, and L-BFGS-B differentiates the misfit by finite differences with a step of 1e-8 in log10: a 1e-13
relative wobble on an MSE of 2e4 is 2e-9 absolute, which is 0.1 units of gradient at that step, enough to change
the line-search step at the 1e-8 level, after which the two paths are different optimizer runs. The step-3 tests
were not run in a Slurm job after step 3 landed (the Phase 3 test jobs ran before it; `03_performance.md` section 2.2
claims bit identity from tests that pass on the Rome login nodes), which is how the claim survived until this
addendum's test job on a Genoa node failed it. The Phase 3 forward gate (1e-8 of relief) was never in doubt;
the optimizer gate's premise that step 3 is bit-identical was.

**Consequence.** The Phase 3 AD stage is not a reproduction of v1's L-BFGS-B path, and on AP5 it is not a
reproduction of v1's AD optimum: the gate fails for that stage. The failure is a property of L-BFGS-B's
finite-difference gradients on a misfit surface with more than one basin (the AP5 AD surface has at least two,
MSE 1005 and 2826, both reachable from the same start point), not of the solver's accuracy. Two things follow,
neither done here: (i) any claim that "the AD stage reproduces v1" must say "on GM1 and, with the Phase 2 code, on
AP5"; (ii) the Phase 4 AD profile (`04_optimizer_v2.md`) replaces L-BFGS-B with a one-dimensional profile over
K/D with closed-form D, which does not depend on finite-difference gradients, and its AP5 result decides which
basin is the global one. Whether to restore bit identity in step 3 (a scalar power loop costs about 0.5 s per
build) is Steve's call; it would make the Phase 3 AD path reproducible on Genoa nodes but would not make
L-BFGS-B robust.

## 5. What the evidence supports, and where it is weak

- Measured: every passing comparison in sections 1 and 2 is a direct comparison of run outputs (params.json,
  trajectory.parquet) with the v1 printed values and with each other; the step-3 one-ulp difference is measured on
  a Genoa node (job 51704806) and the power-function difference directly (job 51705914).
- Inferred: that the finite-difference gradient is the amplifier (the paths agree to 1e-13 relative until the
  first post-gradient step and then separate; no other mechanism is present in the code path, which is otherwise
  bit-identical, as the acrit and nonlinear stages show).
- Weak: the AP5 AD surface's structure is known only from two L-BFGS-B paths; the v2 profile (Phase 4) is the
  test. The AP5 nonlinear baseline passed when it finished (filled 2026-11-10). The GM1 AD comparison passes but with a different path, so the
  "Phase 3 reproduces v1" statement for the AD stage rests on one tile and on the Phase 2 code for the other.
